About

I am Zhiying Li, a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at Michigan State University (MSU), advised by Professor Tianxing Li. My research interests lie at mobile and ubiquitous computing, with a particular focus on developing practical sensing systems.

Prior to joining MSU, I earned my bachelor’s and master’s degree from the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), majoring in Electrical Engineering and Applied Mathematics. My past experience includes working as a Machine Learning Engineer Intern at Meta, where I contributed to LLM-based solutions for search ranking. I have also served as a Graduate Teaching Assistant for Mobile Application Development at MSU for multiple semesters, winning 2024 AT&T Excellence in Teaching with Technology Awards.

Publications

  1. MobiSys’26 (Workshop)
    BlinkChat: Blink-Augmented Prompting for Adaptive Human–LLM Interaction
    Zhiying Li, and Tianxing Li
    In Proceedings of the 24th Annual International Conference on Mobile Systems, Applications and Services Workshops 2026
    DOI PDF

    AI Highlights

    • Explores involuntary eye blinks as an implicit input channel, using blink rate as a coarse proxy for cognitive strain to adapt LLM response verbosity.
    • Detects blinks locally and injects them as control tokens into the typing stream, leaving the chat workflow unchanged and sending no raw biometric data to the server.
    • Shortens responses by up to 31.5% at high blink rates in simulations over 400 long-form prompts, strongest on short-answer factual tasks and attenuated on reasoning-heavy ones.
  2. MobiSys ’25 (Poster)
    Poster: MobiChem: A Ubiquitous Smartphone-Based Toolkit for Practical Fruit Monitoring and Analysis
    Zhiying Li, Abdul Aziz, Patrick Phuoc Do, Phuc Nguyen, and Tianxing Li
    In Proceedings of the 23rd Annual International Conference on Mobile Systems, Applications and Services 2025
    DOI PDF

    AI Highlights

    • Uses the smartphone’s own screen as the light source and its front camera as the sensor, enabling hyperspectral fruit analysis without extra active hardware.
    • Adds a zero-power screen cover that filters display light into the wavelength bands needed for hyperspectral sensing, keeping the toolkit low-cost and portable.
    • Estimates maturity, chlorophyll, lycopene, free sugar, and firmness for tomatoes, bananas, and avocados, targeting dietary assessment and food-safety applications.
  3. MobiSys’25
    MobiChem: A Ubiquitous Smartphone-Based Toolkit for Practical Fruit Monitoring and Analysis
    Zhiying Li, Abdul Aziz, Patrick Phuoc Do, Phuc Nguyen, and Tianxing Li
    In Proceedings of the 23rd Annual International Conference on Mobile Systems, Applications and Services 2025
    DOI PDF

    AI Highlights

    • Turns a commodity smartphone into a practical hyperspectral sensing platform using its screen, front camera, and a passive, zero-power optical filter cover.
    • Pairs a CNN with a ranking-based learning technique that shapes the latent space to classify maturity and estimate chemical and physical fruit properties.
    • Achieves 95.67% accuracy for chlorophyll, 98.76% for lycopene, 93.53% for sugar concentration, and 91.34% for maturity classification across 288 fruits.
  4. DigiBiom’22
    SkinProfiler: Low-Cost 3D Scanner for Skin Health Monitoring with Mobile Devices
    Zhiying Li, Tejas Viswanath, Zihan Yan, and Yang Zhang
    In Proceedings of the 2022 Workshop on Emerging Devices for Digital Biomarkers 2022
    DOI PDF Slides

    AI Highlights

    • Targets long-term tracking of skin lesions and moles, a practice recommended for melanoma risk but often neglected for lack of accurate, accessible tools.
    • Clamps an LED ring onto the phone camera to capture 17 photos under varied lighting, then reconstructs 3D skin-surface models with a modified photometric stereo algorithm.
    • Keeps total hardware cost under 20 dollars; a preliminary evaluation verifies the tool’s performance, with evaluation in clinical settings proposed as the next step.
